# Phase 0 Customer Interview Guide

Status: ready to use  
Target: 15–20 qualified interviews  
Audience: product managers, data/analytics professionals, product-minded engineers, designers/researchers, and operators with a current learning need

## Research question

Do product and data professionals have an urgent, recurring need to learn a reasoning-heavy skill for a real work outcome, and will they commit time or money to a product that measures application rather than content completion?

## Qualification

Include participants who:

- Have a specific work decision, project, interview, promotion, or role transition.
- Need to learn or strengthen a complex skill within 2–8 weeks.
- Can describe what failure or success would look like.
- Can realistically spend at least one hour per week.

Do not count:

- People discussing learning only in the abstract.
- People primarily seeking credentials, language learning, K–12 help, or answer generation.
- Friends giving encouragement without a current problem.
- The owner.

## Consent opening

Use this wording:

> Thank you for helping with Filosage research. I want to understand how you currently learn difficult professional skills. This is not a sales call. I will take written notes about the problem and workflow. I will not publish your name, company, or quotes without asking separately. You may skip a question or stop at any time. Is that okay?

Record whether the participant agreed. Do not record audio unless they separately consent.

## Interview structure

Target: 30 minutes.

### 1. Current situation — 5 minutes

1. What are you responsible for in your current work?
2. Tell me about the last time you had to learn something difficult quickly.
3. What triggered the need?
4. What was at stake if you did not learn it?

Listen for an actual event, not an opinion.

### 2. Existing behavior — 8 minutes

1. Walk me through exactly what you did first.
2. What tools, courses, people, or documents did you use?
3. Where did you lose time?
4. What did you abandon, and why?
5. Did you pay for anything? How did you decide?
6. How did you know whether you understood the subject?

Ask for artifacts when appropriate: a search history, notes structure, saved course, document, or work output. Do not request confidential company material.

### 3. Outcome and evidence — 7 minutes

1. What did you need to be able to do afterward?
2. Could you tell the difference between recognizing an explanation and being able to apply it?
3. Did you test yourself in a new situation?
4. Did anyone review your work?
5. What would credible evidence of improvement look like?
6. What would make an assessment feel untrustworthy?

### 4. Urgency and value — 5 minutes

1. How often does this kind of learning need happen?
2. What did the last attempt cost in time, money, delay, or confidence?
3. What happens if the problem remains unsolved?
4. Who controls the budget for learning tools?
5. What would have to be true for a focused 2–8 week learning cycle to be worth paying for?

Do not ask “Would you pay $14.99?” before understanding behavior. Hypothetical agreement is weak evidence.

### 5. Product commitment — 5 minutes

Explain only after discovery:

> Filosage is testing a focused learning cycle that begins with a real work outcome, diagnoses the starting point, uses retrieval and applied practice, and finishes with evidence against a rubric.

Ask:

1. Which part would be most useful?
2. Which part would you distrust?
3. What real outcome would you bring?
4. Would you commit about two hours per week for four weeks using the current public product?
5. What would need to be true for you to start now or pay for Pro when it launches?

Record the commitment:

- No commitment.
- Follow-up requested.
- Agreed to begin a structured learning cycle in the product.
- Agreed to introduce another qualified participant.
- Expressed credible purchase intent.

## Evidence standards

Strong evidence:

- A recent event with consequences.
- Existing paid or time-intensive alternatives.
- Repeated attempts.
- A concrete outcome and deadline.
- Sharing a workflow or non-confidential artifact.
- Committing calendar time to use the product.
- Paying or agreeing to subscribe after seeing the experience.

Weak evidence:

- “That sounds cool.”
- Feature suggestions without a current problem.
- Hypothetical willingness to pay.
- Compliments from friends.
- Waitlist signup without a specific learning need.

## Closing

> Thank you. I will use these notes to decide whether this problem is strong enough to keep investing in. Filosage will contact you again only if you explicitly requested a follow-up or product updates.

## Interview completion rule

An interview counts toward the Phase 0 gate only when:

- The participant matches qualification.
- Consent is recorded.
- A real recent learning event is documented.
- Existing behavior is documented.
- Outcome and urgency are rated.
- Commitment is recorded.
