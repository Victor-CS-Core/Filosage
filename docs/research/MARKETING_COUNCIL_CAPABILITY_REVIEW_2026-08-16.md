# Filosage capability-first marketing council review

> Simulated council — each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review.

**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Input:** `docs/research/PRODUCT_CAPABILITY_MARKETING_AUDIT_2026-08-16.md`
**Question:** How should Filosage position and improve the product after a full capability audit showed that its professional and English-speaking audience statement is narrower than its implemented product?

## Seated council

- April Dunford — positioning chain and competitive alternatives
- Seth Godin — audience, trust, and product remarkability
- Byron Sharp — reach, category entry points, and distinctive assets; designated dissenter
- Alex Hormozi — offer clarity, time-to-value, certainty, and effort
- Claude Hopkins — specific claims, samples, and measurable response

## April Dunford — position from alternatives and value

The product should not begin by naming every person who might use it. It should begin with what those people do instead: ask a generic chatbot for explanations, collect disconnected videos and articles, enroll in a broad marketplace course, build an improvised notes-and-flashcards system, or keep delaying the goal. Filosage's unique attributes are the causal answer to those alternatives: a learner-defined or published course, a structured Capability Cycle, requested course language, connected practice and review, transparent source state, and evidence that separates self-report from observed and assessed work.

Those attributes create one coherent value theme across students and professionals: turn a concrete learning goal into a focused route, practice it deliberately, return before it fades, and inspect what the record supports. Student, personal-project, career, and work situations are segments and entry points within that value, not four unrelated products.

Dunford's warning is that “everyone who learns” is not a useful best-fit segment. Broad product eligibility should coexist with focused campaign positioning. The product can serve independent students and self-directed learners while the first acquisition experiment still targets one measurable cohort. The chosen market category must make the workflow familiar; it should not invent a new category merely because the feature set is unusual.

**Recommendation:** Position Filosage as a self-directed learning and course-creation workspace, then use situation-specific campaigns and flagship courses for coursework or exams, personal projects, career goals, and current work goals.

## Seth Godin — choose the change, not an exclusionary demographic

The useful smallest audience is not automatically “English-speaking product managers.” It is the smallest group united by a meaningful change the product can reliably help create. Here the change is from an important but unstructured learning goal to a focused course, deliberate practice, and honest evidence. A student preparing for an exam, a self-taught person building something, and a professional facing a decision can all want that change.

The current product contains the seeds of remarkability: it creates a course in the language the learner requests, refuses to turn a link into false source proof, asks the learner to commit before revealing feedback, and keeps confidence separate from demonstrated work. Those are product behaviors worth talking about. Hiding them behind a generic professional-skills message makes the product less specific, not more.

Godin would still resist a mass-market “learn anything” promise. The generous message is explicit about fit and limits: individual learners aged 13 or older, an English interface today, multilingual or bilingual course content, no school administration, no credential, and no guaranteed outcome. Permission also matters: launch email consent, optional analytics, and reminders must remain opt-in and truthful.

**Recommendation:** Define the audience by the change they seek and the behavior they value, while preserving focused campaigns and explicit anti-personas.

## Byron Sharp — broaden category entry points and preserve recognition

Sharp would challenge a strategy that fragments Filosage into narrow micro-personas before it has meaningful reach. A new learner does not need to decide whether they belong to a “professional reasoning” tribe. They need Filosage to come to mind in a buying or usage situation: “I have an exam topic I do not understand,” “I want to build a personal project,” “I need a portfolio capability,” or “a work decision depends on learning this.” These are category entry points.

The app should create broad mental availability across those situations with consistent distinctive assets: Filosage, the coral curiosity signal, the Course Deck, the Capability Cycle, the course-outcome proof, and the evidence-state language. Segment examples can change while those assets remain stable. Separate visual identities or mutually exclusive audience pages would weaken recognition.

Sharp's dissent is that a knowledge-worker-only beachhead can become a permanent reach ceiling if it controls the home page, metadata, prompts, and course defaults. That is exactly what happened. Acquisition experiments may be targeted, but the base product should remain easy to recognize and relevant to all eligible category buyers.

**Recommendation:** Use four broad learning-situation entry points and one consistent brand system; do not let a campaign cohort redefine the entire product.

## Alex Hormozi — make the offer concrete and reduce uncertainty

The broad audience correction is useful only if the offer becomes clearer. “AI-assisted learning” is not an offer. The concrete sequence is: bring a goal or choose a published course, get a focused route, complete deliberate practice, and leave with an inspectable record. Plus buys private course creation. Pro buys advanced portable evidence and publishing. Free proves the learning workflow on published courses.

The weakest value-equation variable is perceived likelihood. The product has source states, criterion-bound assessments, and evidence separation, but prospects cannot yet see a complete evidence report without already having a course and learner state. A clearly fictional public evidence example and a controlled flagship course reduce uncertainty more effectively than stronger adjectives.

Students and self-directed learners also have different buying power and eligibility. Free can serve eligible learners aged 13 or older; paid plans are currently 18+, individual, United States only, and closed. That commercial boundary should shape conversion experiments without changing who the product can help. Do not manufacture urgency, guarantees, or bonus stacks while demand and outcome evidence are absent.

**Recommendation:** Lead plans with the job each tier completes, show the product proof, and keep quotas, eligibility, and closed billing explicit.

## Claude Hopkins — let the product sample and measure the response

The earlier audience statement failed Hopkins's standard because it was an opinion repeated as fact. The repair is not another committee-approved sentence. It is a set of specific claims tied to behavior and small tests.

The public course outline is a useful sample, but it does not demonstrate multilingual creation, adaptive review, flashcard control, or the evidence report. Add only samples that the product can prove: a configured flagship course, a fictional evidence demonstration, and role-neutral course-creation examples. Every surface should have a measurable response tied to a real decision: discovery, course opening, verified practice, meaningful return, contextual plan interest, or evidence engagement.

Hopkins would also require separate tests for the four learning situations. Do not call them validated audiences because code supports them. Run small campaigns or interviews with keyed entry points and compare qualified course starts, first completed practice, and return behavior. The language claim should be tested with actual non-English and bilingual course acceptance, not only English copy describing the feature.

**Recommendation:** Replace assumptions with specific capability claims, product samples, and segmented response tests before broad promotion.

## Where the council disagrees

1. **Smallest audience versus broad availability** — Godin and Dunford want a focused best-fit cohort; Sharp rejects allowing that cohort to cap reach. The resolution for Filosage is to keep the product contract broad while campaigns and flagship courses remain focused. Evidence that would change this: qualified activation, first-practice, and return rates by entry point.
2. **What should lead the offer** — Hormozi favors private course creation and portable evidence because they create obvious paid value; Godin favors the learner's transformation; Dunford insists both must derive from alternatives. The resolution is a transformation-led core promise with tier-specific capability proof.
3. **How prominent multilingual creation should be** — Godin sees it as remarkable; Hopkins wants acceptance evidence; Dunford treats it as an attribute whose value depends on the segment. The resolution is to state it accurately and visibly, test it in course creation and discovery, and avoid a universal quality or localization claim.
4. **How many messages to run** — Sharp wants stable category entry points and assets; Hopkins wants segmented tests. The resolution is one core brand and product story with four keyed situation examples, not four separate brands.

## Chair's synthesis

### Recommended positioning contract

**Working category:** Self-directed learning and course-creation workspace.

**Core promise:** Turn a goal you care about into a focused course, deliberate practice, and evidence you can inspect.

**Language proof:** The interface is English today. When course creation is available, the learner can request a specific course language or bilingual pairing, and generated instruction is checked against that request.

**Audience:** Independent students aged 13 or older, self-directed learners, career changers, working professionals, and individual course creators. Campaigns may focus on one cohort at a time. Schools, teams, under-13 users, credential seekers, and people seeking autonomous high-stakes advice are outside the current fit.

**Competitive alternatives:** Generic chat, disconnected tutorials and videos, broad course marketplaces, improvised notes or flashcards, and delaying the goal.

**Value themes:**

1. Focus the learning goal.
2. Practice instead of only consuming.
3. Learn and create in the requested course language.
4. Return when evidence and timing say it is useful.
5. Inspect what the record proves and what remains pending.

### Tier story

- **Free:** Learn from published courses and keep practice, notes, progress, review, and available course-grounded study tools connected.
- **Plus:** Create private courses and custom study tools for a goal that the published library does not cover.
- **Pro:** Add advanced portable evidence, revocable sharing, cross-attempt analysis, and course publishing.

Billing activation, deployment, publication, and broad promotion remain outside this review.
