# Founding Launch Catalog

Status: Phase 0 curriculum specification  
Initial catalog focus: product and data work situations; this is a bounded campaign/catalog choice, not the product eligibility boundary
Catalog rule: small enough to review, connected enough to compound

## Curriculum thesis

The first catalog should teach professional reasoning that transfers across tools and employers. It should not chase rapidly changing software tutorials or compete with credential libraries.

Every flagship course must include:

- A concrete learning outcome tied to a real study, personal, career, or work situation.
- Prerequisites and a starting diagnostic.
- Named misconceptions.
- At least one realistic work artifact.
- Independent transfer tasks.
- A capstone rubric.
- Source provenance.
- A review owner and next review date.
- A clear statement that internal evidence is not an accredited credential.

## Shared concept spine

The initial courses should reuse and deepen:

- Claims and evidence.
- Measurement and operational definitions.
- Uncertainty.
- Causality and confounding.
- Feedback loops.
- Incentives and second-order effects.
- Decision criteria.
- Tradeoffs.
- Communication of limits.

This shared spine becomes the first Mastery Graph domain.

## Flagship pathway

### Make a Defensible Product Decision

Outcome:

> Diagnose a product decision, identify the evidence and uncertainty that matter, choose an action, and explain the tradeoffs in a decision memo.

Suggested sequence:

1. Frame the actual decision.
2. Separate observations, assumptions, and preferences.
3. Define useful measures.
4. Identify causal traps.
5. Map feedback and second-order effects.
6. Compare options under uncertainty.
7. Write the decision memo.
8. Defend and revise the recommendation.

Capstone artifact: a decision memo for a real or safely anonymized product situation.

## Twelve-course catalog

### 1. Systems Thinking for Product Decisions

- Artifact: feedback-loop map and intervention memo.
- Core misconception: every visible problem has a local linear cause.
- Review expertise: systems thinking and product strategy.

### 2. Practical Statistics for Product Teams

- Artifact: analysis note explaining what a result does and does not support.
- Core misconception: statistical significance equals practical importance.
- Review expertise: applied statistics.

### 3. Designing Trustworthy Experiments

- Artifact: experiment brief with hypothesis, unit, metrics, risks, and stopping logic.
- Core misconception: randomization removes every interpretation problem.
- Review expertise: experimentation.

### 4. Reading Metrics Without Fooling Yourself

- Artifact: metric critique and revised measurement plan.
- Core misconception: a dashboard metric is the underlying reality.
- Review expertise: analytics and measurement.

### 5. Causal Reasoning for Product Decisions

- Artifact: causal diagram and identification-risk note.
- Core misconception: association plus a plausible story establishes causality.
- Review expertise: causal inference.

### 6. Product Strategy From First Principles

- Artifact: strategic choice memo with explicit exclusions.
- Core misconception: a roadmap or goal list is a strategy.
- Review expertise: product strategy.

### 7. Writing Testable Product Hypotheses

- Artifact: testable hypothesis set tied to a decision.
- Core misconception: any prediction is a useful hypothesis.
- Review expertise: product discovery and experimentation.

### 8. Data Storytelling for Decisions

- Artifact: decision-focused narrative with one defensible visual.
- Core misconception: more charts create more credibility.
- Review expertise: analytics communication and information design.

### 9. AI Literacy for Product and Data Professionals

- Artifact: capability and risk assessment for an AI-supported workflow.
- Core misconception: fluent output implies reliable understanding.
- Review expertise: applied AI and risk.

### 10. Evaluating AI Features and Models

- Artifact: evaluation plan with task set, metrics, failure analysis, and release gate.
- Core misconception: one aggregate benchmark predicts product usefulness.
- Review expertise: model evaluation and product engineering.

### 11. Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

- Artifact: option comparison with assumptions, reversibility, and information value.
- Core misconception: uncertainty should be eliminated before acting.
- Review expertise: decision analysis.

### 12. Capstone — Diagnose a Real Product Decision

- Artifact: complete decision package.
- Core misconception: finishing component lessons proves integrated competence.
- Review expertise: cross-functional panel.

## Production order

Phase 1 should build:

1. Make a Defensible Product Decision.
2. Systems Thinking for Product Decisions.
3. Reading Metrics Without Fooling Yourself.
4. Designing Trustworthy Experiments.

The remaining catalog stays specified but ungenerated until the first pathway passes review and produces real learner evidence.

## Expert review rubric

Each reviewer scores 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accuracy | Critical errors | Minor or ambiguous issues | Accurate within scope |
| Outcome alignment | Content-first | Partial connection | Every task supports outcome |
| Source quality | Missing or weak | Mixed | Appropriate primary/authoritative sources |
| Misconception quality | Strawman | Plausible but weak | Consequential and well corrected |
| Transfer | Repeats example | Surface change | Meaningfully new context |
| Rubric | Vague | Partly observable | Observable and discriminating |
| Professional realism | Toy task | Simplified | Credible work artifact |
| Accessibility | Blocking issues | Minor issues | Meets intended standard |

No public flagship course ships with:

- Any zero in accuracy, source quality, or accessibility.
- A total below 13/16.
- Unresolved critical reviewer feedback.

## Rights and provenance

- Prefer primary and authoritative sources.
- Record title, publisher, URL, access date, and relevant scope.
- Do not copy protected course material.
- Expert reviewers confirm their contribution and publication permission.
- Every public course shows AI-assistance and review status.
